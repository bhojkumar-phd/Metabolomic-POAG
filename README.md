Metabolomics metadata for the glaucoma aqueous humor study is in this file.
Summary: This study identified significant metabolic changes in the aqueous humor of glaucoma patients compared to healthy individuals, revealing dysregulation in carbohydrates, lipids, DNA components, amino acids, vitamins, and polyamines. Notably, agmatine and thiamine were decreased in glaucoma patients. In a mouse model of retinal neuroinflammation, both agmatine and thiamine treatments, delivered via eye injections or topical application using extracellular vesicles, reduced inflammation and protected retinal ganglion cell function. These treatments also protected photoreceptor cells from oxidative stress in lab experiments. The findings suggest that agmatine and thiamine could be potential neuroprotective therapies for glaucoma by targeting neuroinflammation.
